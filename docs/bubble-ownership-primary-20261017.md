# Balloon ownership: primary-source candidates — 2026-10-17

## Decision

Start with a private ownership replay using the **already present ogkalu balloon boxes**. The local comparator retained class-0 balloon proposals even though its grouping path used only class-1/2 text boxes. This can test whether a uniquely predicted container helps distinguish neighboring text units without downloading weights or running another detector. It cannot establish a balloon contour, a speaker, semantic importance, a glyph mask or permission to erase artwork.

If that replay shows that rectangular containers are the remaining limitation, the smallest documented mask candidate here is **huyvux3005's YOLO11n balloon segmenter**, available through a separately published ONNX export. Acquisition, graph inspection, license/provenance review and real-page validation would precede any experiment with that export. **kitsumed's YOLOv8m segmenter** is a reserve comparison with an existing ONNX artifact and weaker documented training provenance. These are three candidate families, selected for their output contracts, not popularity.

This note used public source/model metadata and read-only local comparator source. No private image, private report, key, Vault or story memory was read. No inference, package installation, new model acquisition, paid request, production change or packaging occurred. The single local model file was hashed without loading a runtime. The supplied failure context—connected white paper overmerging balloons/asides and a CTD+PP union losing to CTD—is motivation, not a fresh result established here.

## 1. Ogkalu text and balloon detector: useful boxes already available

The owning card declares three **location** labels: `0: bubble`, `1: text_bubble`, `2: text_free`. Its accompanying configuration names `RTDetrV2ForObjectDetection`, with the same label mapping; this is object detection, not an instance-mask head. The card describes the original RT-DETR-v2 r50vd trained on roughly 11,000 comic images, at 640 pixels. That description does **not** document the architecture/training of the later small `detector-v4-s_int8.onnx` export or establish equivalence to the original model. In particular, do not assign the original model's parameter count or accuracy to the small export. [Owning card](https://huggingface.co/ogkalu/comic-text-and-bubble-detector), [configuration](https://huggingface.co/ogkalu/comic-text-and-bubble-detector/blob/main/config.json).

The author's pinned ONNX adapter actually separates label 0 into `bubble_boxes` and labels 1/2 into `text_boxes`. It uses a 640×640 RGB stretch, float32 `/255`, `orig_target_sizes=[[width,height]]`, and outputs `labels, boxes, scores`; its CPU settings use four intra-op threads and one inter-op thread. It then delegates grouping to other code. Thus “bubble” is verified in the adapter, rather than inferred from the repository name. The adapter also has slicing/download machinery that should not be inherited into the offline reader incidentally. [Publisher adapter at `8977b91a4f7a40c3917c5a268e9e7d78e1d818da`](https://github.com/ogkalu2/comic-translate/blob/8977b91a4f7a40c3917c5a268e9e7d78e1d818da/modules/detection/rtdetr_v2_onnx.py).

## 2. Huyvux3005 Manga109 balloon segmentation: mask alternative

The producer identifies a YOLO11n **instance-segmentation** model, based on `yolo11n-seg.pt`, trained at 1600×1600 with one speech-bubble class. It supplies boxes and instance masks. The producer's checkpoint metadata scan lists Ultralytics `SegmentationModel`, `Segment` and `Proto` classes; these corroborate a segmentation head, rather than a plain box-only detector. The producer names MS92/MangaSegmentation and Manga109 as training data. Its card's aggregate metrics and unspecific CPU timing do not establish accuracy, latency, RAM use or joined-balloon ownership on this reader's pages. [Producer card](https://huggingface.co/huyvux3005/manga109-segmentation-bubble), [checkpoint metadata](https://huggingface.co/huyvux3005/manga109-segmentation-bubble/blob/main/best.pt).

The independent **exporter** owns the evidence for its published ONNX conversion; it is not an independent accuracy validation of the producer. It declares unchanged weights, opset 17, a 1024-square letterboxed input and class `0: balloon`. Published outputs are `(1,37,21504)` box/score/mask coefficients and `(1,32,256,256)` prototypes. Decoding requires NMS, per-instance mask reconstruction, cropping each mask to its own box, undoing letterboxing and mapping into source pixels. A global prototype map is not an instance mask. The exporter shows CPUExecutionProvider usage; graph compatibility and parity remain unverified locally. [Exporter contract](https://huggingface.co/mednasserallah/manga109-segmentation-bubble-onnx).

An instance mask is a more direct boundary hypothesis than connected white paper or a bounding rectangle. This is an inference from its output type, not evidence that the model reliably separates touching/joined balloons. Neither card gives a subtype error breakdown for open/frameless balloons, thought clouds, black bubbles, caption boxes, handwriting or SFX. One balloon class provides no distinct narration/thought/SFX/irrelevance decision.

## 3. Kitsumed YOLOv8m speech-bubble segmentation: reserve

The publisher supplies a segmentation checkpoint and a dynamic-shape ONNX export. Its training configuration explicitly has one class, `0: speech bubble`, and a YOLOv8m-seg training example at 640 pixels. The checkpoint scan identifies a `SegmentationModel`, `Segment` head and `Proto` block. The card warns that mask boundaries can be wavy. Thus this is a real container-mask candidate, with no evidenced glyph-mask or semantic-role head. [Owning card](https://huggingface.co/kitsumed/yolov8m_seg-speech-bubble), [class/training configuration](https://huggingface.co/kitsumed/yolov8m_seg-speech-bubble/blob/main/config.yaml), [checkpoint metadata](https://huggingface.co/kitsumed/yolov8m_seg-speech-bubble/tree/main).

The published ONNX avoids requiring a fresh pickle conversion, but its actual names/shapes, opset, custom domains, CPU compatibility and peak memory were not inspected locally. Dynamic input support does not establish correct arbitrary-size preprocessing or better small-text recall. Dataset identity, split/duplicate handling, rights inventory, held-out performance and subtype behavior are unknown in the inspected card/configuration. Do not infer support for narration, open or joined balloons, thoughts, black balloons or SFX from the single class label.

## CPU feasibility and loading boundaries

Ogkalu has both an owning CPU adapter and an already pinned local ORT contract. Huyvux3005's exporter supplies an ORT CPU example. Kitsumed supplies ONNX but no inspected CPU benchmark. ORT officially supports a CPU execution provider; that is a runtime capability, not proof every proposed graph executes unchanged. Model-file bytes are not process RSS or activation memory. No result here establishes the 16 GB/no-discrete-GPU reader's time or RAM targets. [ORT execution providers](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/).

For any future mask experiment, inspect graph input/output metadata, opset, external-data references and operator domains first; allow only the pinned reviewed runtime/graph, bound page/input/mask counts, and reject required custom libraries. The producer PT files are pickle-based checkpoints with detected executable class references, so a conversion should not be triggered in the reader. Prefer a separately reviewed immutable ONNX file; verify its actual hash before loading. Published model-loading examples can download models/assets and save images—do not import those behaviors. Inference must accept local verified files, keep raster data in RAM and fail offline without metadata lookup. These are experiment boundaries, not claims that ONNX alone proves a model safe.

Its merger builds graph components from textline geometry/font size/direction and splits them using distances; its `utils/bubble.py` checks black/white ratios near a crop edge and explicitly describes failures on solid backgrounds, contours and connected bubbles. ## One bounded experiment: reviewed ownership on the existing private pack

1. **Freeze inputs before inspecting scores.** Use only the existing eligible development pages and matched retained source planes. Verify source checksums, page/cutoff, exact recipe/model hashes, original CTD/PP member polygons, original unit boundaries and reader exclusions. Keep all originals immutable. Do not read gold transcripts to select geometry, recover missing members from union boxes, or transplant another recipe's members. Missing required records is an explicit stop for that page, not permission for another download.
2. **Add private ownership labels.** A reviewer marks each retained text member's container identity, `none` or `ambiguous`, and whether an existing unit already spans owners. Mark the visible original balloon/container boundary where practical, independently of detector output. Record adjacent, joined, frameless/open, thought, black, narration/scene-text and handwritten-aside cases separately; SFX and unknown role remain valid visible text. Audit labels, duplicates, coverage and rights. OCR transcription labels do not supply container ownership or clean-background ground truth.
3. **Run one numeric replay, no inference.** Extract valid label-0 proposals from the existing compatible ogkalu evidence at its recorded ≥0.3 threshold. Preserve all scores/classes/float geometry and rejected records. Propose an owner only when every original member of the unchanged unit lies fully inside exactly one predicted balloon box, no competing balloon touches any member, and no exclusion is crossed; otherwise return `unknown/review`. Do not pick the highest-score owner, enlarge a box, merge boxes or infer a contour from white paper. A proposal's unique *predicted* box is still a hypothesis: an outside aside can lie inside its rectangle. Human ownership labels decide whether it was actually correct.
4. **Compare relations before crops.** Against the frozen CTD grouping and the existing white-component ownership, report correct owner relations, false cross-balloon relations, missed ownership, abstentions and coverage of small/balloonless text. Include per-case failures and any existing unit that needs splitting. Keep per-line/per-unit identities and candidate relations as numeric review evidence; do not automatically split/merge original units or concatenate transcripts. If several independent units share a valid owner, preserve their boundaries and original order. A confirmed common container is not automatically a single utterance or speaker.
5. **Stop and inspect.** Any loss of source members/exclusions, invented bindings, new forced ownership or art change fails the replay. An ownership improvement alone does not establish an OCR or translation improvement. Only a separately scoped subsequent OCR ablation with unchanged recognizer/crop settings could measure that. For this replay measure elapsed geometry time and peak process RSS on the target CPU; do not report it as detector or end-to-end latency, extrapolate a population p95 from a tiny pack, or claim the MVP acceptance targets.

All proposals stay review-only: no cleanup/erasure, renderer change, story-memory fact, automatic relevance filtering, new cloud request or model download. Unknown speakers remain unknown. Existing captured-source bindings, cutoff/evidence, reader-locked names/pronouns and explicit exclusions survive independently of transient caches. No metadata/model lookup is allowed in offline execution. This experiment can establish whether existing boxes contribute useful ownership evidence; it cannot establish safe reconstruction of the artwork.
